METHOD FOR INCOMPLETE PROBLEMS

Impossible Problems Lab

“Impossible” usually means the evidence is incomplete, the variables cross disciplines, or the next useful measurement has not been identified. The laboratory records how far a problem can actually be advanced.

01 — Problem

State the practical question without embedding the desired answer.

02 — Available evidence

List records, measurements, observations, source quality, dates, and access limitations.

03 — Unknown variables

Name missing quantities explicitly. Do not replace absence with convenient defaults.

04 — Assumptions

Separate measured values, inferences, scenario ranges, and administrative choices.

05 — Methods attempted

Preserve equations, prompts, scripts, tools, and parameter sets used.

06 — What failed

Record dead ends, unstable results, false positives, implementation mismatches, and why they failed.

07 — What worked

Describe reproducible advances without inflating them into total solutions.

08 — What the result does not prove

State causal, predictive, legal, scientific, or operational conclusions that remain unsupported.

09 — Next evidence requirement

Identify the single measurement, record, experiment, or source most likely to reduce uncertainty.

10 — Reproducible files

Publish code, configuration templates, ledgers, tests, and ordinary export formats where safe.

11 — Next justified step

Recommend only an action supported by the current evidence and operating limits.

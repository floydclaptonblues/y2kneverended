# Public release editorial notes — October 2026

These notes are separate from the original research reports, which are publicly mirrored unchanged for provenance. They are not a new numerical experiment.

1. **37.5°S westward crossing:** One 6-hour particle (ID 219) released at 37.5°S / 94°E crossed west of 80°E on day 363. No corresponding 3-hour crossing appears from 37.5°S. Some sentences in the archived `CLOSURE_INTERPRETATION.md` and `FULL_YEAR_NUMERICAL_COMPARISON.md` incorrectly state that no release at or south of 36.5°S reached a western gate in *either* run. The table and the public page preserve the actual 6 h / 3 h counts.

2. **Not strictly monotone:** First passage west of 80°E by latitude in the 6-hour calculation is 15%, 5%, 0%, 1.7%, then zero southward. This is a broad latitude-dependent tendency, not strict monotonic decline, and not evidence for a material transport barrier.

3. **Southward movement versus qualifying criterion:** For the 3-hour cartwheel-band western gate-crossers, two particles made small southward excursions (~0.09° and ~0.11°). Neither met the study's prespecified 0.5° excursion threshold. The defensible statement is that **none** completed the *qualifying substantial southward-then-westward sequence*; not that every one moved exactly zero degrees south.

4. **Eddy residence:** Returning within 50 km of an earlier modeled position after 30 days is a useful recurrence indicator, but it does not alone establish trapping by a coherent eddy. Numerical path identity and residence fractions were not both demonstrated converged across multiple step refinements.

5. **Numerical check:** The 3 h / 6 h comparison shows aggregate stability under the one tested timestep refinement; it does not prove a numerical limit or resolve individual model tracks. The G0 reference is conditional and not a confirmed impact location.

6. **Censoring:** Domain exits are not beachings. Masked-field exits inside the domain may be coast-related or field artifacts. The source data end at ~119.92°E; possible subsequent transport is unknown.

The historical GLORYS drift experiment surveyed ocean transport only. No posterior over MH370 impact positions, debris-specific recovery likelihood, or search recommendation is claimed.
